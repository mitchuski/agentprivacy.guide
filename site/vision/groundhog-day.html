Groundhog Day strategy session — the Hitchhiker / Game of 42 project's annual February 2nd event for institutional partners, Patron Fish and the AGM money push.

A strategy conversation defining "Groundhog Day" — the Hitchhiker / Game of 42 project's annual February 2nd event that "flips the script" from the gamer crowd toward institutional, Davos-suit-wearing partners and their risk managers. Framed around the Earth OS partnerships theme, the event is pitched as the project's own decentralized, local "un-Davos": the venue where serious money people, family offices, funds, academics, lawyers and insurers are courted, where last year's Patron Fish commitments are converted into money actually in the bank, and where the business, financial, legal and IP house is put in order for the AGM. The team agrees it is the moment the project stops feeling like "just an art event" and presents its seriousness on stage — solid business plan, solid financial plan, a demonstrated capacity to raise money — while bringing in De-Sci and academic guides to translate top-tier thinking into solutions for institutional-grade problems (AI, governance, bioregional issues). It also carries a deliberate "dark side": guarding against bad money, frontier-lab approaches and equity grabs, leaning on serious advisors and Vision Fish to stay disciplined. The whole thing is framed as rebooting the Earth operating system, with success measured concretely in dollars raised and the number of real money-people actually pitched to.

Key points

  • Audience flip: Groundhog Day targets institutional, Davos-suit partners and their risk managers, plus curious observers who watched the Hitchhikers from afar but lacked formal justification to get involved; this is their venue.
  • Patron Fish day, scaled up: building on the earlier first two test Patron Fish (the website approach), this is where commitments become money received in the bank; the explicit goal is to go big and pitch to far more money people.
  • The annual rhythm: every February 2nd, review accounts, finances, code, legal and IP, then tweak/reform the business strategy for the coming year; it doubles as internal mice prep for the AGM.
  • Decentralized / un-Davos: rather than only attending Davos, run their own local Groundhog Day via business dinners and gatherings, inviting people to invest as the project grows.
  • De-Sci and academic angle: present top-quality academic thinking applied to institutional/Davos-grade problems (AI, governance, bioregional pieces); a quiet February is also a good slot for an academic conference examining the crypto-economic protocol, the Hitchhiker economy and the legal/technical infrastructure.
  • People to involve: a lot of Robbie and his first Vision Fish picks; a sharp, dedicated fundraiser hire; business schools (e.g. an art/media/business school in Lyon); City of London think tanks, insurance companies, and friendly law/insurance firms as advisors or sponsors.
  • What changes after: the project feels formal, serious and AGM-ready — not just an interesting art project but a credible business capable of raising money, putting that seriousness on stage.
  • The not-for-profit Hitchhiker model: can turn over hundreds of millions and attract billions from family offices, but shareholders don't get indefinite exponential returns; money is destined to build the commons of the Earth operating system.
  • Risk / dark side: building earlier-spotted risks (e.g. the Nov 5th lottery-hack pilot finding) into a stronger plan; preparedness for serious capital and frontier labs sniffing around, including saying no to too much money or equity. Bad money is bad money.

Action items

  • Design and test the Groundhog Day format collaboratively before going to the money people, so the pitch is ready when the opportunity comes.
  • Convert prior Patron Fish commitments into actual funds received; go big on Patron Fish outreach to family offices, funds and institutions.
  • Hire a dedicated, sharp fundraiser specifically for these institutional rooms.
  • Run the annual February 2nd review of accounts, finances, code, legal and IP; reform the business strategy plan for the next year.
  • Host local un-Davos business dinners and partner meetings to invite investment.
  • Engage Robbie and his first Vision Fish picks; approach business schools (Lyon), City of London think tanks, insurance companies and law firms for advice, sponsorship or logo placement.
  • Consider an academic conference in the quiet February window to examine the crypto-economic protocol, Hitchhiker economy and legal/technical infrastructure.
  • Present prior-year fixes (e.g. the lottery-hack remediation) to crypto-economic and business partners as proof of strengthening; do back-end privacy/security/legal cleanup.
  • Be deliberate about where money comes from — vet sources, lean on serious advisors and Vision Fish, and be prepared to decline bad money or equity-heavy capital.
  • Measure success by money raised and the number of real money-people actually pitched to — not just by publishing a website and hoping.
