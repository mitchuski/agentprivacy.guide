How to read the sphere

  1. A star is a page. Each one is a guide page, read by a small local language model () and turned into a direction on a sphere.
  2. Six readings place it. That direction is scored against six short sentences, one per axis of the lattice: 🛡️ protection · 🤝 delegation · 📜 memory · 🔗 connection · ⚡ computation · 💎 value. The six scores, through the star's own projection, are where the star sits.
  3. Snap to a vertex. The sign of each reading is one bit; six bits are one of the 64 vertices. Snapping pulls every star onto its vertex.
  4. The torus seats it differently. The torus places a page by its declared posture, or else by its site's strand: where it lives, not what it says. Switch to the torus seat and watch every star move to the vertex the torus gives it.
  5. One site at a time. Pick a site, or the harness lanes together (harness · lexon · fieldguide · vpk), to see how one collection spreads across the text readings.
  6. What a host would see. Rotate the six axes by a secret key: every distance between pages survives, the seats do not. A host can still match pages without learning where they sit.
